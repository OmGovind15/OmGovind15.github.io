Modeling Affective Distress from Video: A Modality-Aware Multi-Branch Learning Approach

Published in Proceedings of the 28th ACM International Conference on Multimodal Interaction (ICMI '26)

This research presents MAMBL, a modality-aware Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) framework for automated affective distress and anxiety assessment from clinical interview video feeds. We identify a fundamental temporal-structural mismatch across behavioral modalities: facial Action Units (AUs) represent sparse, high-frequency micro-expressions, whereas head pose and gaze dynamics exhibit continuous, autocorrelated momentum. To respect these distinct inductive biases, MAMBL introduces decoupled branch architectures—a 1D CNN for sparse AU events and a bidirectional LSTM for smooth rigid head and eye movements.

The representations are unified via a gated temporal attention pooling mechanism and gradient-based spatial saliency. Evaluated on a clinical Trier Social Stress Test (TSST) dataset, MAMBL achieves 71.04% classification accuracy and competitive performance across StressID, UBFC-Phys, and AVEC 2017 benchmarks.

Teacher-Guided Discriminative Flow Matching for Latent Representation Learning

Accepted at Northern Lights Deep Learning Conference (NLDL 2027)

This work introduces Discriminative Flow Matching (DFM) for robust latent representation learning. We train a time-dependent neural vector field to transport latent representations along straight-line probability paths between a source noise prior and class-conditional target representations supervised by a momentum-updated exponential moving average (EMA) teacher network.

By incorporating a learned scalar gating parameter, DFM dynamically balances continuous vector field alignment with discriminative supervision. Empirical evaluation demonstrates superior classification accuracy, well-separated latent cluster geometries, and substantially lower Expected Calibration Error (ECE) compared to standard cross-entropy and self-supervised contrastive baselines.

CA-AFP: Cluster-Aware Adaptive Federated Pruning for Heterogeneous FL

Under Submission at ACM IKDD CoDS-COMAD 2026

CA-AFP is a clustered dynamic sparse federated learning framework engineered for resource-constrained edge computing environments experiencing severe statistical (non-IID) and system heterogeneity. We integrate a dynamic "Prune-and-Heal" parameter exploration with trajectory-cosine client clustering, enabling client groups with similar data distributions to collaboratively discover personalized sparse network topologies.

Our hybrid importance scoring mechanism evaluates weight magnitude, intra-cluster coherence, and gradient consistency during iterative pruning and regrowth. Evaluated across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, FEMNIST, and WISDM datasets, CA-AFP maintains robust model performance at up to 70% parameter sparsity, significantly reducing communication overhead while improving fairness (minimizing variance in client accuracy).

Predictive Modeling in ODI Cricket: Training Strategy, Feature Engineering, & Explainability

Under Review (Revision Stage) at International Journal of Sports Science & Coaching (SAGE Publications)

This research develops a comprehensive machine learning framework for predicting match outcomes in One Day International (ODI) cricket across 1,394 historical matches (2002–2023). To address realistic operational deployment and eliminate temporal data leakage, we implemented a rigorous chronological expanding-window cross-validation strategy.

We engineered granular ball-by-ball and over-by-over situational features and benchmarked multiple models including Logistic Regression, SVM, Random Forest, XGBoost, and Deep Neural Networks. Our findings establish that a Unified Inning Model performs best in the first innings where win probabilities evolve smoothly with overall run-rate and historical team form. Conversely, the second innings is governed by volatile chasing dynamics, where our proposed adaptive Over-Wise Model Switching strategy achieves an outstanding 84.6% prediction accuracy. Game-theoretic SHAP interpretability analysis quantitatively illustrates how feature importance transitions across key phases of the game.

Image Analysis with SIFT, BoW, and HOG

This project delves into classical feature-based image classification methods. We implemented the Scale-Invariant Feature Transform (SIFT) to extract robust keypoints from images, which were then clustered into a "Bag of Visual Words" (BoW). By creating histograms from these visual words, alongside Histogram of Oriented Gradients (HOG) features, we built a powerful model for classifying images. This work is fundamental to understanding the building blocks of many modern computer vision systems.
